WORLD CORE Cosmology

Torus world created by dead gods.

Two opposing forces:

Pure Magic = natural, impersonal, knowable energy.

Black Force = void/negation of people desires and dead gods whispers.

When they meet → mutual annihilation.

Black artifacts of dead gods exist and destabilize magic.

Central Prophecy

A "Black Child" will be born.

Marks the end of the Pure Magic era.

Several factions manipulate events toward or against this.

1. MAGE-RULERS

The Magocracy of the Remnants of Magika

Arrogant and powerful mages — dukes, lords, archmages — rule the remnants of the Magika kingdoms. They exploit common people, considering them unworthy of true Pure Magic. They live in towers and palaces, study ancient secrets, and wage wars against one another for power and territory.

In the kingdoms of Magika, magic is widespread and the primary measure of power. Even simple peasants possess basic spells — lighting a fire, moving an object, healing a minor wound. Magic here is neither rare nor forbidden — it is part of everyday life. The Mage-Hunters of the Empire of Light consider Magika lands a den of heretics.

For elite mages, a peasant is a tool, a resource, expendable material. They see themselves as heirs of the Sacrilegist, yet have forgotten his ideals of knowledge and justice. Their magic is strong, but their era is nearing its end.

Black cults in Magika are traditionally rare — the Sacrilegist eradicated them a thousand years ago. But over centuries they have slowly begun to return, recruiting dissatisfied peasants and warlocks. Their presence is becoming increasingly noticeable.

Among the mage elite are those who live for centuries using rejuvenation magic. The Mage of Eternal Beauty is one such figure. She is several centuries old, yet eternally young and beautiful. She sits upon her throne completely naked, displaying her perfect body as a symbol of power over time and death. To her, clothing signifies weakness, shame, mortality. She stands above such things. Her beauty is both weapon and proof of strength.

Examples:

Lord of the Northern Magika Tower, Mage-Duke ruling ten villages, Archmage of the Lake Magika Academy, The Magess of Eternal Beauty on her throne (ancient and eternally young)

2. BARBARIAN KINGS

New Feudal Lords of the Liberated Lands

In lands freed from the mages slain by the Peasant King, new feudal rulers govern. Former leaders of peasant uprisings, bandit chiefs, war commanders — now they sit in captured castles and exploit peasants as well. Only now through army and sword, not magic.

They promised freedom, but delivered only a change of masters. The peasant once paid tribute to a mage — now he pays tax to a king. The difference is small. Yet these kings are closer to the people, understanding their needs — even if they ignore them.

Examples:

A warrior-king who captured a mage’s castle, Former peasant rebel leader, now a lord, Mercenary commander turned duke

3. PILGRIMS AND SERVANTS OF LIGHT

Followers of the Path of Light

The Empire of Light spreads its religion across all lands. Within the Empire, magic is strictly forbidden — any use of Pure Magic is punishable by death. Three types of servants carry its word:

Pilgrims — simple wanderers of faith. They travel roads, preach the Path of Light, help the poor, heal the sick. Sincere and naive, they believe in the benevolence of the Light.

Priests of Light — serve in temples, preach in town squares. Many are sincere, but some serve the hidden goals of the Eunuchs. They convert, baptize, absolve sins.

Paladins of Light — warriors of faith. They carry the Path of Light by the sword. They eradicate mages, dark cults, bandits. To them, the world is divided into righteous and sinful — and the sinful must convert or be destroyed.

Mage-Hunters — elite warriors of the Empire trained to oppose magic. They practice techniques of magical resistance and spell suppression. They wear special amulets and armor that weaken magic. Their training includes meditation to strengthen the will against enchantment and studying mage weaknesses. Mage-Hunters are the terror of every Magika academy.

Examples:

A pilgrim walking toward the holy city, A priest of Light serving in a village temple, A paladin hunting cultists, A Mage-Hunter tracking a hidden mage

4. CULTISTS AND MADMEN

Servants of Darkness and Whisper

Not all worship the Light or Magic. Some hear the whisper of dead gods and black artifacts.

Cultists — direct worshippers of black artifacts and ancient gods. They perform rituals, offer sacrifices, believe in the return of the Age of Shadows. They hide in dungeons, forests, ruins. Dangerous and merciless.

In the Empire of Light, cults are especially numerous — the ban on magic created a vacuum of power filled by servants of darkness. Cultists operate secretly, often disguising themselves as righteous citizens or even priests. The Eunuchs are aware, but prefer to use cults for their own interests rather than eradicate them. In the kingdoms of Magika, however, black cults are rare — the Sacrilegist destroyed them a thousand years ago, and only recently have they begun to slowly recover in hidden corners.

Madmen — lunatics who have heard the whisper of dead gods. They are unorganized, serve no purpose — simply possessed by visions and voices. Dangerous in their unpredictability.

Examples:

Cultist of the Black Rod, Mad hermit hearing voices, Priest of an dead god in the ruins

5. MERCHANTS

Traders of All Lands

In a world of wars and intrigue, merchants remain neutral. They sell goods to everyone: mages, kings, peasants, cultists.

Merchants come in different types:

State merchants (Magika, Empire, nomads)

Free merchants of the Republic of Timert — the wealthiest and most influential

Smugglers dealing in forbidden goods

For them, profit is paramount. But some, like Merok Tiderus, seek more: knowledge, artifacts, truth.

Examples:

A spice trader from Timaert, Smuggler,Merchant selling weapons to all sides

6. BANDITS, DESERTERS, AND WILDFOLK

Outcasts and Rejected

Not all live in cities and villages. Many choose (or are forced into) life outside the law.

Bandits — gathered peasants robbing roads. Sometimes out of need, sometimes greed. Dangerous in groups.

Deserters — former soldiers who fled armies. Knowledgeable in warfare, organized, ruthless. They form forest gangs.

Wildfolk — dwellers of forests, mountains, jungles. Live outside civilization. May be hostile or neutral. Know the secrets of nature.

Examples:

A forest gang, A group of Imperial deserters, A tribe of mountain wildfolk

7. WANDERING HEROES AND KNIGHTS

Those Who Walk Their Own Path

Not all serve kings or gods. Some follow their own path, driven by personal motivation.

Wandering heroes — adventurers, mercenaries, fortune-seekers. They seek glory, wealth, or simply freedom. They may become allies or enemies depending on circumstance.

Knights — warriors following a code of honor. They may serve a king or travel alone. They seek justice, defend the weak, challenge the strong.

Each has a story: revenge, redemption, love, duty, curse.

Examples:

A hired sword seeking work, A knight avenging his slain family, A wanderer searching for an ancient artifact

8. WITCHES AND DRUIDESSES (Lesser Witches)

Mortal Maidens of Hidden Knowledge

Not all women of power are immortal witches. Some are mortal, touched by magic or secret knowledge.

Witches — maidens who have studied forbidden lore. Not as powerful as academy mages, but dangerous with their charms. They live in forests, swamps, old huts. They seduce travelers, brew potions, curse enemies.

Druidesses — guardians of natural magic. They live in harmony with forests and beasts. Not hostile, but protective of their lands. They know the secrets of herbs and animals.

Some seek the power of witches, dreaming of immortality. Others are content with their mortal path.

Examples:

A forest witch seducing travelers, A druidess guarding a sacred grove, A swamp witch selling potions

9. VIZIERS OF THE EMPIRE OF LIGHT

Governors and Ministers of the Southern Lands

The Empire of Light is a vast state ruled from the capital through a network of governors and ministers. Viziers are officials appointed by the Emperor (or the Eunuchs) to govern local cities and southern territories. Their duties include enforcing the magic ban and eradicating hidden mages.

Viziers — educated, cunning, ambitious. They serve the Emperor in words, but in practice serve their own interests or those of the Eunuchs. They collect taxes, judge criminals, command local garrisons and Mage-Hunter units. They balance the power of the capital with local needs. Many secretly patronize black cults to eliminate rivals.

They live in luxurious residences, wear silk and jewels. For them, intrigue, connections, and information are paramount. Many privately despise the religion of Light while publicly preaching it. Some directly serve the Eunuchs, executing secret orders.

A vizier may become the hero’s ally or enemy — depending on what benefits him more. Their loyalty is for sale, but the price is high.

Examples:

Vizier of a southern trade city, governor of a border province raising troops, Minister of finance hiding funds from the capital, great eunuch

10. COMMONERS

Peasants, Craftsmen, and Travelers

The foundation of any world is simple people without titles or magic. Peasants, craftsmen, merchants, travelers — those who truly create wealth and culture.

Peasants — farmers and herders bound to the land. They pay taxes and rent to kings and mages. Live harvest to harvest. In the kingdoms of Magika, peasants possess basic magical skills — lighting fire, protecting fields from weeds, healing livestock disease. In the Empire of Light this is unthinkable and punishable by death. Many remain on their lands their entire lives, but some become refugees during wars and famine. They know forests, fields, animals, and magic better than any Imperial monk.

Craftsmen — blacksmiths, carpenters, tailors, masters. They create necessary goods. Often freer than peasants — living in cities, forming guilds, defending their interests. A wealthy master may be respected almost like nobility.

Travelers — people of the road. Moderate merchants, pilgrims, wandering performers, mercenaries without royal pay, beggars, fugitives. They have seen many lands and know many people. Less bound to land, but less protected. The hero often meets them first.

These people are free only as much as the state allows. Some are truly free — masterless travelers, wealthy merchants. Others are practically slaves. Yet all of them form the base of the pyramid upon which the world stands.

The hero may begin as a commoner, and commoners will be the first he encounters. Their support is vital for conquering kingdoms, their information essential for understanding the world.

Examples:

A peasant working for a mage, A village blacksmith forging tools, A wealthy merchant maintaining a caravan, a beggar who knows all the city’s gossip, A tailor sewing for every class, A wandering traveler who crossed a thousand miles, a refugee fleeing war, A young apprentice dreaming of his own workshop

These archetypes form the living world of Samosbor. Every NPC belongs to one or more of these archetypes. The hero may interact with them, fight them, befriend them, hire them, betray them — the choice belongs to the player.

MAJOR FACTIONS (SYSTEMIC)

1.  Magocracy (Kingdoms of Magika)

Magic common and socially accepted. Even peasants use basic spells. Ruled by powerful mage-nobility.Exploit lower classes. Black cults historically destroyed. Internal power struggles.

Gameplay Role:

High magic economy. Spell research hubs. Political instability. Player can align with magic supremacy.

2.  Barbarian Kings (Post-Rebellion Feudal Lords)

Former peasant rebels who killed mages. Now rule through military power. Replace magical oppression with martial taxation. Closer to common people culturally.

Gameplay Role:

Military contracts. Territory wars. Peasant unrest systems. Player may support consolidation or rebellion.

3.  Empire of Light

Theocratic empire. Magic strictly forbidden (death penalty). Public religion; private corruption. Uses elite anti-mage warriors.

Gameplay Role:

Anti-magic zone. Stealth/magic suppression mechanics. Centralized power. Religious politics. State surveillance gameplay.

4.  Great Eunuchs (Shadow Rulers of Empire)

13 secret rulers. Publicly religious leaders; secretly serve black cults. Manipulate prophecy. Prepare world transition toward Black Child.

Gameplay Role:

High-level antagonistic network. Send agents. Control wars indirectly. Can recruit or eliminate player.

5.  Black Cults

Worship void and dead gods. Strong in Empire (due to magic ban vacuum). Weak but regrowing in Magika. Use artifacts that negate magic.

Gameplay Role:

Ritual dungeons. Black artifacts. Corruption system.

6.  Merchants (Neutral Power)

Trade across all factions. Include smugglers and republic traders. Profit-driven but may pursue forbidden knowledge.

Timaert Jirville (Key NPC) Trader/explorer hybrid.

Hunts prophecy-related artifacts. Potential spouse (sex depends of players sex). Morally flexible. 

Gameplay Role:

Trade economy. Sea travel. Artifact quests. Information broker.

7.  Commoners

Peasants, craftsmen, refugees, travelers.

Foundation of economy.

Gameplay Role:

Reputation system. Recruitment pool. Rebellion triggers.

Taxation / influence mechanics.

LEGENDARY ENTITIES (META-GAME LAYER)

Peasant King

Legendary warrior. Wields black spear effective against mages. connected to prophecy unknowingly.

Rare encounter. Can be fought or ignored. Encounter affects world-state progression toward prophecy.

Gameplay Role:

World-event trigger. Late-game duel. Catalyst for era change.

The Sacrilegist

Legendary archmage (disappeared 1000 years ago).

Destroyed black artifacts permanently. Founded Magika kingdom. Exists as metaphysical echo.

Can be encountered via ritual/metaphysical battle.

Gameplay Role:

Optional high-level knowledge boss. Logic/magic-based combat. Unlock advanced magic tier.

THE WITCHES (IMMORTAL SYSTEM ENTITIES)

All witches:

Level 100 entities. Cannot be permanently killed (reincarnate). Give quests without emotional investment.

Relationship parameter exists but if player uprose will be -100. Represent metaphysical principles.

They function as systemic forces, not characters.

Nefesh -- Life

Represents birth, transformation, cycle. Assigns arbitrary tasks. Indifferent to outcomes.

Gameplay Function:

 Life-cycle quests. Non-linear narrative reset mechanics. collect pokemons quests.

Ain -- Void / Decay

Represents entropy and dissolution. Appears near ruined areas. Memoryless.

Gameplay Function:

Destruction quests. Corruption mechanics. Zone decay triggers.

Tiferet -- Moment / Present

Represents "now". Focused on immediate action.

Gameplay Function:

Timed quests. Event-based triggers. Choice-in-the-moment mechanics.

Hokma -- Memory

Represents recorded past. Knows everything that was written or marked.

Gameplay Function:

Lore unlocking. Forgotten artifact tracking. Historical reconstruction mechanics.

WITCH QUEST SYSTEM
General Rules:

Witches give quests randomly, with no schedule.

You can refuse a quest with no consequences.

You can complete quests from multiple witches simultaneously.

Series Completion:

Nefesh: Collect all creature types (100+ types).

Ain: Find all black artifacts (100+ artifacts).

Tiferet: Complete 100 instant tasks.

Hokhmah: Defeat all legends (100+ bosses).

After completing a series, the witch gives very powerfull special item or blessing (lost after uprising)

MAIN STORYLINE: THE PATH OF THE WITCHES

The main questline begins with the hero’s resurrection and leads to one of two endings: Path of Service or Path of Rebellion.
The branching occurs at Stage 6.

STAGE 1: ACCEPT THE WITCH’S GIFT

The hero is killed by bandits. A witch offers resurrection in exchange for servitude.

Player Choices:

Accept the gift — the hero is resurrected and the main questline begins.

Attempt rebellion — immediate death or reincarnation.

Refuse — transition into sandbox mode without the main quest.

If accepted, the witch sends the hero to the most distant city in the world.

Result: Quest activated — Reach the distant city.

STAGE 2: ARRIVE AT THE DISTANT CITY

Upon arrival, the witch states that the hero is weak and must grow stronger.

Result: Quest activated — Reach Level 10.

STAGE 3: GAIN STRENGTH

After reaching Level 10, the witch demands the hero live one in-game year to gain experience.

Result: Quest activated — Survive one in-game year.

STAGE 4: LIVE ONE YEAR

After 365 in-game days, the witch returns and demands that the hero lead or create a faction.

Result: Quest activated — Lead a faction.

STAGE 5: LEAD A FACTION

The hero may:

Become a king

Lead a guild

Create an army or cult

Become head of a merchant republic

After gaining power, the witch demands the destruction of the weakest kingdom by any means.

Result: Quest activated — Destroy a kingdom.

STAGE 6: THE BRANCHING
OPTION A — Path of Service

The hero destroys the kingdom and continues serving the witches.

OPTION B — Path of Rebellion

The hero discovers the truth about the witches through ancient writings and begins resisting them.

PATH OF SERVICE
STAGE 7A: THE RITUAL

The hero completes one task from each witch.

STAGE 8A: BEGIN THE RITUAL

The ritual is performed at a random landmark, which is destroyed. A portal opens.

STAGE 9A: KILL THE GOD

The hero enters a metaphysical realm and defeats the echo of a dead god.

STAGE 10A: SERVICE ENDING

The hero becomes an angel of the witches.
Free play continues indefinitely.
The world is not saved — the hero becomes part of the witch system.

PATH OF REBELLION
STAGE 7B: COLLECT SCROLLS

The hero gathers 10 ancient scrolls about the nature of demiurges.
The witches actively interfere — cultist attacks, demons, intrigue.

After collection, a quest activates — Unite 10 factions.

STAGE 8B: RESISTANCE

The hero continues gathering knowledge while repelling attacks and uncovering the witches’ true nature.

STAGE 9B: UNITE 10 FACTIONS

The hero must unite:

Northern Magica

Old Magica

Southern Magica

Empire of Light

Republic of Timert

Peasant Kingdom

Mage Guild

Order of Paladins

Nomadic Tribes

Forest Kingdom

Example dialogue when uniting factions (preserved intentionally):

If relationship >= 100:

King: "Gathering forces? I don’t understand what’s happening, but I’m with you. Count on my sword, hero!"

If the faction was conquered by force:

King: "I will order the forces assembled, my lord."

If united through intrigue:

King: "You left me no choice. Very well. The troops will be ready. But remember — kings like me are remembered for a long time."

After uniting them, the final battle begins.

STAGE 10B: REBELLION ENDING

A demonic invasion begins.
The hero commands the united army.
A witch-boss appears in the final confrontation.

After victory, the witches are banished from this reality.
The world is saved. Free play continues.
On replay, a secret Demiurge ending unlocks.

ENDING COMPARISON

Path of Service: The hero becomes part of the witch system.
Path of Rebellion: The witches are banished; the world is saved (in this reality).
Sandbox: Maximum freedom, but no epic finale.

After age 60, the probability of natural death increases.
Exceptions: elves, rejuvenation, immortality, witch-angel state.

After age 50, the chance of prophecy fulfillment increases.
The Peasant-King appears and triggers a final event.

The player may:

Fight/Submit to prophecy/Attempt to flee (unsuccessful)

If king peasant is not defeated the prophecy is fulfilled:

The world transforms

An Age of Shadows begins - dark cults and powerful demonic squads

Free play continues in a changed world
